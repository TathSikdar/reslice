# ADR-006 — The 3D view is the fourth pane, and the slab moves onto the other three

## Context

FR-610 as written says "the 3D view shall be selectable as a layout", and the Phase 2 spec
flags that this needs a control that does not exist: a layout selector, offering something
like *2×2 MPR*, *MPR + 3D* and *3D only*. That is how commercial workstations do it, and it
is the obvious answer.

Phase 1 §1.6 is the constraint on the other side. Every visible control has to be
explainable to the bottom in a ten-minute demo, and a control that exists because other
products have one is exactly what that rule is for. A layout selector would be the only
control whose whole purpose is to rearrange other controls rather than to change what is
on screen.

The first attempt made the 3D view a fourth *entry* in the dropdown that chose what the
slab pane showed — *MIP · MinIP · Average · 3D* — so the two views shared a cell and only
one was on screen at a time. That removed the new control but bought a worse problem: the
3D view and the slab projection are the two things a reader most wants to look at
together, and the dropdown made them mutually exclusive.

## Decision

The 3D view **is** the fourth pane. It is always on screen; nothing switches it away.

The slab projection moves onto the three planar panes, where MIP, MinIP and Average are
chosen by one dropdown and thickness is Shift+wheel over a pane. Thickness starts at
**off**, where each pane is exactly the single-plane reconstruction it has always been.

No layout selector, no full-window 3D mode, no second arrangement of panes. FR-203's
double-click maximize already covers "I want to see this one bigger".

## Consequences

**A slab was always a property of a plane, not of a pane.** This is the part that makes
the change a simplification rather than a rearrangement. A slab MIP is "collapse *n*
millimetres either side of this plane"; the plane it is centred on is the pane's own. A
dedicated slab pane duplicated the axial plane in order to project through it, which is
why the old fourth pane was constructed as `Axial, isSlab: true` — a copy of pane one with
a flag. Deleting it deletes the copy, and the three panes gain a capability they should
always have had: a coronal MIP, which the old design could not produce at all.

**Thickness off is the default, so nothing changes until asked.** At zero the panes call
`PlaneRenderer` exactly as before. The dropdown then names what *would* happen rather than
what is, which is a mild cost and the alternative was worse — starting at 20 mm would have
opened the viewer on three projections nobody asked for.

**The Hounsfield readout and the measurement tools switch off while a slab is open.** One
rule, `MainViewModel.IsProjecting`, replaces the old per-pane `IsSlab` flag. A projected
pixel is the brightest thing somewhere within the slab and has no one depth, so a number
labelled with the crosshair's position would be a number about a different point. The old
design enforced this by having a pane that could not measure; the new one enforces it by
having a state in which no pane can. Scroll the thickness back to zero and everything
returns.

**The export needed one change.** FR-409's fourth target used to name a `ViewportViewModel`;
the 3D pane has none, because it has no reslice plane. `ExportTarget.IsVolume` names it
instead, rather than a null meaning both "the whole grid" and "the 3D pane".

**Two controls became one.** The shading checkbox is gone and shading is always on. An
unshaded volume rendering is a picture nobody would choose, so the checkbox had a right
answer and a wrong one, which is a default rather than a choice.

**FR-610 is satisfied differently from how it is written.** The requirement says "as a
layout"; this delivers "as the fourth pane", which meets the intent — the 3D view is
visible and exportable — and not the letter. FR-201 and FR-207 are amended in the Phase 1
spec to match, rather than left disagreeing with the code.
