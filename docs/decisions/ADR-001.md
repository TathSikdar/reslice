# ADR-001 — Split FR-107 into a malformed-header check and a gantry-tilt check

## Context

FR-107 says to reject a series with a "non-orthogonal `ImageOrientationPatient`", and
describes that as the gantry-tilt check. Those are two different things, and the
requirement as written can never fire on a tilted series.

`ImageOrientationPatient` (0020,0037) holds two direction cosines: the direction of a
row of pixels and the direction of a column, both in patient coordinates. Tilting the
gantry rotates the whole image plane. The two cosines rotate together and stay
perpendicular to each other. Their dot product is zero before the tilt and zero after
it, so a test on their orthogonality is blind to precisely the condition it is named for.

What a tilt actually does is separate the plane's own normal (`row x column`) from the
direction the slices step in (`IPP_1 - IPP_0`). On an untilted series those two vectors
are parallel. On a tilted one they diverge by the tilt angle.

Appendix C of the spec describes it this way. Section 4 does not. The spec contradicts
itself, and following §4 literally would have shipped a validator that passes every
tilted study.

## Decision

Two requirements instead of one.

- **FR-107a — malformed orientation.** Reject when `|row . column| > 1e-6`. This is a
  corrupt or hand-edited header, not a scanner geometry. Real acquisitions are always
  orthogonal.
- **FR-107b — gantry tilt.** With `n = normalize(row x column)` and
  `d = normalize(IPP_1 - IPP_0)`, reject when `|d . n| < 1 - 1e-3`.

Both tolerances are constructor parameters on `GeometryValidator`, not constants. The
1e-3 figure corresponds to about 2.5 degrees and is a calibration knob: it has been
exercised against synthetic tilt only, and wants tuning against a real tilted series.

## Consequences

- The two failures get distinct messages. "Your header is malformed" and "your scanner
  was tilted" send a user to different places, and merging them would have made the
  rejection banner useless in the one situation where it matters.
- The validator gained an ordering constraint: FR-107a runs before anything that uses
  the normal, because `row x column` is meaningless if the inputs are not perpendicular.
  A skewed header first surfaced as `InconsistentGeometry` for exactly this reason.
- The traceability matrix records FR-107a/b as replacing FR-107, with the reason. The
  spec is not silently overridden; the disagreement is on the record.
- Handling tilt properly means resampling onto a sheared grid. That is out of scope
  (§1.4), so tilt is a rejection rather than a correction.
